Product Catalog and Inventory Conventions (E-Commerce Domain Knowledge)

SKU and Variant Modeling
A product (e.g. "T-Shirt") is distinct from its purchasable variants (e.g. "T-Shirt, Size M,
Color Blue"). Each variant must have its own unique SKU (Stock Keeping Unit) code, its own price
(variants may differ in price), and its own stock count. The parent product record holds shared
information (title, description, category, images common to all variants), while each variant
holds the attributes that differ (size, color, stock, SKU, price delta).

Reserved vs. Available Stock
Total stock for a variant should be tracked as two numbers: "on-hand" (physically in the
warehouse) and "available" (on-hand minus quantity already reserved by carts/pending orders that
have not yet shipped). When a customer adds an item to their cart, or completes checkout but
before fulfillment, that quantity should be reserved (deducted from "available" but not from
"on-hand") so two customers cannot both purchase the last unit. Reservations that are not
converted to a completed order within a defined window (e.g. cart reservation expiring after 15
minutes, or order payment window expiring after 30 minutes) must be released back to available
stock.

Backorder Handling
When a variant's available stock reaches zero, the system may still allow ordering it as a
"backorder" only if the merchant has explicitly enabled backorders for that product. In that
case, the customer must be clearly shown an estimated availability/ship date, and the order
must be flagged internally as a backorder so fulfillment does not attempt to ship it before
restocking.

Price History and Currency
Product prices change over time (promotions, cost changes). The system should retain a price
history so that a completed order always shows the price the customer actually paid, even if the
product's current listed price later changes -- an order's recorded price must never be
recalculated retroactively from the catalog's current price. All prices must be stored with an
explicit currency code, not assumed from a single default currency, especially once the system
supports more than one storefront region.

Category and Search Indexing
Every product should belong to at least one category for browsing, and its title/description
should be indexed for keyword search. Out-of-stock products should still be searchable and
viewable (so customers can see they exist and get notified when restocked) but should be clearly
marked unavailable rather than hidden entirely, unless the merchant explicitly wants a product
delisted.
