User Account and Authentication Conventions (E-Commerce Domain Knowledge)

Account Lockout After Failed Attempts
To prevent brute-force password guessing, an account should be temporarily locked after a
defined number of consecutive failed login attempts (commonly 5). Once locked, further login
attempts should be rejected for a cooldown period (e.g. 15 minutes) or until the account owner
resets their password, whichever comes first. The failed-attempt counter must reset to zero after
a successful login. Lockout messaging shown to the user should not reveal whether the email
address itself is registered, to avoid account enumeration.

Password Reset Token Expiry
When a user requests a password reset, the system issues a single-use, time-limited reset token
(commonly expiring after 30-60 minutes) sent to the account's verified email address. The token
must be invalidated immediately after first use, and must not be reusable even if the reset flow
is interrupted partway through. Requesting a new reset token should invalidate any previous
unused reset token for that account.

Email Verification Gating Checkout
Depending on the business model, an unverified email address may still be allowed to browse and
add items to a cart, but placing an order (checkout) with a newly registered account should
generally require email verification, since order confirmations, shipping updates, and receipts
depend on a reachable email address. If verification is required before checkout, the customer
must be given a clear, immediate way to resend the verification email without losing their cart.

Session and Token Expiry
Authentication sessions (whether cookie-based or JWT-based) should have a defined expiry, and
sensitive account actions (changing password, changing payment methods, viewing full order
history with addresses) should ideally require a recently-authenticated session rather than an
old, long-lived token. Logging out on one device should not silently invalidate sessions on other
already-logged-in devices unless the system explicitly implements "log out everywhere."

Role Separation
Customer accounts and admin/staff accounts should be modeled as distinct roles with distinct
permission sets. An admin managing the product catalog, orders, or refunds must not use the same
authentication/authorization path as a customer browsing and checking out, even if both flows
share the same underlying user-identity system.
