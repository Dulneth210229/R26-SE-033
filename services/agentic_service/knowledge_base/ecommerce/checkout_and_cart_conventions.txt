Checkout and Cart Conventions (E-Commerce Domain Knowledge)

Guest Cart Merge on Login
When a guest user (not signed in) adds items to their shopping cart and later logs in or
registers, the guest cart must be merged with any existing saved cart already associated with
that account. Items that exist in both carts should have their quantities summed, capped at
available stock. The guest cart must never simply replace or discard the account's existing
cart contents, since that silently deletes items the user intentionally saved earlier.

Abandoned Cart Handling
A cart is considered abandoned when it has not been modified or checked out within 24 hours of
the last item being added. Abandoned carts should trigger a reminder notification (email or
in-app) to the account holder, if one exists. Abandoned carts should not be deleted automatically;
they should be retained for at least 30 days so the customer can resume checkout later.

Guest Checkout
Guest checkout must be supported for e-commerce systems unless the business explicitly requires
account creation. When a guest checks out without an account, the system must still capture a
valid email address and shipping address, and should offer the option to create an account from
the same details after the order is placed, without asking the user to re-enter information.

Stock Re-Validation at Checkout
Because time passes between a product being added to a cart and the user completing checkout,
stock levels may have changed (other customers may have purchased the remaining stock). The
system must re-validate stock availability for every line item in the cart immediately before
payment is authorized. If any item is no longer available in the requested quantity, checkout
must be blocked for that item specifically (not the whole cart) and the customer must be shown
which item is affected and offered the choice to reduce quantity, remove the item, or continue
without it.

Cart Quantity Limits
Each product in a cart should be capped at either the item's configured maximum-purchase-quantity
(if the merchant sets one, e.g. for limited promotional items) or the currently available stock,
whichever is lower. Attempting to add more than the allowed quantity should not silently fail --
the system should clearly tell the customer the maximum quantity currently allowed for that item.
